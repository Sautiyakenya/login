Founding & Formation

Why SKK exists

Clergy and church leaders from across Kenya's 43 counties at an SKK national assembly

Sauti Ya Kanisa Kenya (SKK) emerged as a pivotal response to the challenges facing the Kenyan church landscape, particularly following the tragic Shakahola massacre and the subsequent discourse on government regulation of churches. Recognizing the urgent need for self-regulation, unity, and structured leadership within the Christian community, SKK was formed to represent and empower the clergy and church leaders across Kenya.

Under the visionary leadership of Prof. Obed Bett Chelogoi, more than 15,000 clergy members from 43 counties united to contribute their insights and recommendations. This massive consultation yielded over 6,000 recommendations, consolidated into a 51-page document formally presented to the Presidential Task Force on Church Regulation — making SKK a recognized voice for the Kenyan church community.

Building on this foundation, SKK developed a more extensive 200-page blueprint outlining the organization's vision, mission, governance structure, and strategic plans, officially launched on 29th September 2023.

Mission & Vision

Mission

To provide leadership, support, and resources that foster cooperation, spiritual vitality, empowerment of Christian leaders and meaningful relationships.

Our Philosophy

Divine Speed

The philosophy of Divine Speed underpins Sauti Ya Kanisa Kenya, reflecting a commitment to advancing God's work with urgency, purpose, and unwavering faith. It emphasizes the importance of aligning with God's timing and direction to achieve extraordinary results within a short period — rooted in the belief that when God's grace is present, what would normally take years can be accomplished in moments.

Governance

Organizational Structure

SKK's governance framework cascades from national leadership down to village facilitators, ensuring accountability and local presence at every level.

1
National Leadership
Strategic direction and overall governance.
2
Regional Leadership
Oversight of multiple counties grouped regionally for coherence.
3
County Leadership
Coordination and implementation of programs within counties.
4
Sub-County Leadership
Localized supervision and engagement.
5
Ward Leadership
Direct interface with community leaders and congregations.
6
Village Facilitators
Grassroots agents overseeing church activities and facilitating communication.
